### 2. **What is Deconvolution?**
In signal processing, deconvolution is the process of reversing the effect of convolution — commonly used to recover an original signal from a convolved (blurred/noisy) version. In audio:
- It may be used for impulse response estimation, dereverberation, source separation, or inverse filtering.
- Often operates in time or frequency domain (e.g., Wiener deconvolution, least-squares deconvolution).
- Not inherently tied to *music semantics* (so unlikely MIR), nor to *filter bank construction* (so not filter_bank), nor to *general-purpose transforms/I/O* (like STFT, resampling, etc.).

However, deconvolution is a **low-level signal processing primitive**, often used as a building block in higher-level algorithms (e.g., in dereverberation, system identification, or blind source separation).
