### 2. **What do these features represent?**
These functions compute well-known spectral descriptors:
- **Spectral flatness**: measure of noisiness (used in MIR & speech)
- **Spectral flux**: change in spectral magnitude over time (onset detection)
- **Spectral rolloff**: frequency below which a certain % of energy lies (timbre)
- **Spectral centroid**: “brightness” of sound
- **Spectral spread, skewness, kurtosis**: higher-order statistics of spectrum
- **Spectral entropy**: disorder of spectrum
- **Spectral crest**: peak-to-average ratio
- **Spectral slope/decrease**: linear/log decay of spectrum
- **Spectral bandwidth**: width of spectrum
- **Spectral RMS / energy**: power-related measures
- **Spectral HFC (High-Frequency Content)**: onset detection
- **Spectral novelty**: used for beat/onset detection (explicit enum types like `SpectralNoveltyMethodType`)
- **PD/CD/WPD/NWPD/RC D**: variants of phase-based descriptors (e.g., phase deviation, complex domain features)

These are **not** low-level transforms (like STFT, FFT, resampling), nor are they high-level music semantics (like pitch estimation, HPSS, time-stretching). Instead, they are **mid-level feature extractors** that operate on pre-computed magnitude/phase spectra (e.g., `mDataArr` is likely a spectrogram matrix: `nLength × mLength` frames × bins).
